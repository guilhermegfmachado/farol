Profile 03

Dyspraxia

Developmental coordination disorder

How it shows up in class

  • Handwriting that is difficult, slow, and often illegible — from genuine motor difficulty, not carelessness.1,2
  • Difficulty with the spatial organisation of the notebook, managing materials, and sequences of steps.3
  • May seem "clumsy" — spills cups, bumps into furniture, struggles in sport.1
  • Disproportionate fatigue after tasks requiring sustained fine motor coordination.
  • Cognitive ability is usually intact — hence the frequent frustration between knowing and being able to show it.1

What it isn't

  • It is not laziness or a lack of effort.1
  • It is not just poor handwriting: the clinical term is Developmental Coordination Disorder (DCD), and it affects planning and motor sequencing, not only writing.2,3
  • It is not solved by "practising handwriting more": effective intervention is task-oriented, not rote repetition.2,4

Strategies without AI

  • Allow the use of a keyboard/tablet for longer pieces of writing.2
  • Reduce mechanical copying from the board — provide notes where relevant.2
  • Provide explicit checklists for multi-step tasks.3
  • Assess the work by its content, not its physical presentation.2

Talking to parents

  • Explain what dyspraxia is in plain language — most parents/guardians have never heard the term: "it is a neurological difficulty with coordinating movement, not carelessness or a lack of effort".
  • Illegible handwriting is the biggest point of friction with parents/guardians — showing that assessment now values content over form helps ease anxiety on both sides.
  • Suggest practical tools for home: voice dictation on the phone for notes, a keyboard for longer written work.
  • If there is a history of "he/she is lazy" or "does not try" in previous years, name it directly: "I know you have heard that before — my reading is different".

Signs to refer for assessment

  • Total refusal to produce written work.
  • Social isolation in physical-activity contexts.
  • Signs of somatised anxiety before written assessments — psychosocial distress is part of the picture.2
