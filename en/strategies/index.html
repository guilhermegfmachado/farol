Autism Spectrum

  • Give advance warning of any change to routine, however trivial it may seem — visual supports and anticipation are empirically supported. universal
  • Turn implicit social rules into explicit ones (what is done, what is not, when). universal
  • Allow sensory breaks in a quiet space when needed. selective
  • Use the focused interest as an entry point into curricular content — a strength, not a distraction. selective

Dyslexia

  • Increase letter and line spacing: there is experimental evidence that wider spacing improves reading in dyslexic children, with no prior training. Evidence for special “dyslexia” fonts is, by contrast, weaker and inconsistent. selective
  • Allow silent reading before any reading aloud — never ask a student to read without warning. universal
  • When the aim is not to assess writing, assess content separately from spelling. universal
  • Offer the same information in multiple formats (audio, video, diagrams). universal

Dyspraxia

  • Allow the use of a keyboard/tablet for longer pieces of writing. selective
  • Reduce mechanical copying from the board — provide notes where relevant. selective
  • Provide explicit checklists for multi-step tasks. universal
  • Assess the work by its content, not its physical presentation. universal

EAL Learner

  • Provide materials with key vocabulary translated into the home language. selective
  • Assess knowledge through visual or practical demonstration wherever possible. universal
  • Pair the student with a bilingual classmate for informal mediation at critical moments. selective
  • Accept early work with grammatical errors — correct content, not form, in the first phase. selective

Giftedness

  • Always have extension tasks ready — not more of the same, but deeper or applied material. selective
  • Allow independent investigation of topics that interest the student within the curriculum. selective
  • Assess by challenge, not by ease — ask for more demanding work in exchange for less volume. selective
  • Acknowledge and validate emotional sensitivity without pathologising it. universal

ADHD

  • Break instructions into pieces: one at a time, written on the board, with a set time — this reduces the load on working memory. universal
  • Allow legitimate movement (handing out materials, cleaning the board, running errands). selective
  • Reduce competing stimuli (a seat away from the window, the door, the noisiest group). selective
  • Immediate, frequent positive reinforcement: in ADHD, sensitivity to reward is altered (delay aversion), so immediate rewards work better than deferred ones. selective

Dyscalculia

  • Keep concrete materials and visual representations (number lines, blocks, diagrams) available without stigma — the move from concrete to abstract happens in explicit stages. universal
  • Allow a number-facts table or a calculator when calculation is not the point of the task — assess reasoning separately from arithmetic. selective
  • Reduce the load: fewer, well-chosen exercises with extended time — speed is not the goal. selective
  • Say the steps out loud and leave a worked example visible during the task — explicit instruction, with modelling. universal
