Ethics & GDPR

The checklist every AI tool must pass before entering the classroom

Privacy & GDPR

  • Minors' data are subject to reinforced protection (Art. 8 GDPR). Consent from parents/guardians is mandatory.1
  • Check where the tool stores data — European servers, non-European, anonymised or not.1
  • Never enter full names, addresses, ID numbers, or clinical diagnoses into generative AI tools.1,3
  • Distinguish institutional use (the school signs a contract) from a teacher's personal use (individual responsibility).1

Algorithmic bias

  • Systems trained on predominantly English-language data may perform worse in languages other than English and in multilingual classrooms.4
  • Automated recommendations can amplify stereotypes of gender, ethnicity, or class — check them critically.4
  • "Academic risk prediction" models are particularly problematic — they frequently reproduce existing inequalities rather than identifying needs.4

Cognitive dependency

  • The goal is to increase the student's autonomy, not to replace the cognitive work that develops it.5
  • Distinguish an access tool (text-to-speech for a student with dyslexia) from a substitution tool (ChatGPT writing the essay).3
  • Assess regularly: is the student developing competence, or developing dependence on the tool?5

The decision checklist

  • 1. Does the tool solve a real barrier, or create a shortcut that bypasses the learning?3
  • 2. Is the data entered protected, and does consent exist?
  • 3. Will the tool's output be checked by a human before it reaches the student?2
  • 4. Is there a non-AI alternative that works equally well?
  • 5. If the tool disappeared tomorrow, would the student be left stranded?
