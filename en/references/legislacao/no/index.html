Legal framework — Norway

The Education Act of 2023, in force since August 2024, and individually adapted education in Norway

This card covers Norway. It is in English because Norwegian is not among the manual’s 24 languages — the Norwegian terms are given as they appear in the law. Ireland is on the default English card, Malta in the Maltese version; separate cards cover Türkiye, Iceland, Serbia and North Macedonia.

A new Education Act — and the end of “special education”

  • The Education Act (opplæringslova, LOV-2023-06-09-30) was passed in June 2023 and has applied since 1 August 2024, replacing the 1998 Act.1
  • It removes the term spesialundervisning (special education) deliberately: the word was judged stigmatising and to contribute to exclusion.3
  • In its place stand three things — individuelt tilrettelagt opplæring (individually adapted education), fysisk tilrettelegging (physical adaptation) and personleg assistanse (personal assistance).3
  • Chapter 11 governs all of this, and for the first time writes the mandate of the educational-psychological service into the Act itself.1,2

Adapted teaching is for everyone; individual adaptation is a right

  • Tilpassa opplæring — adapting ordinary teaching to the pupils in front of you — is a general duty owed to every pupil. It is not a measure, and it needs no decision.2
  • A pupil who cannot get satisfactory benefit from ordinary teaching has a right to individually adapted education (§ 11-6). That does require a formal decision.1

Who decides — PP-tjenesten

  • The PP service (pedagogisk-psykologisk teneste, PPT) produces the sakkyndig vurdering — the expert assessment a decision must be based on.1
  • PPT must assess the pupil’s whole education offering, including the need for physical adaptation and personal assistance — not the teaching alone.1
  • Worth knowing: under the new Act the municipality and PPT weigh more heavily in shaping the provision, and pupils and parents less than before.3

The plan and the yearly account

  • The school draws up an individuell opplæringsplan (IOP) setting out the goals, the content and how the education is to be carried out.1
  • Once a year the school writes an account of the education the pupil actually received and an assessment of progress against the IOP’s goals.1

For quick reference

  • Central text: opplæringslova (2023), chapter 11 — in force 1 August 2024.1
  • Right to individually adapted education: § 11-6, on an expert assessment from PPT.1
  • Individual plan: IOP, with a written yearly account.1
  • The word “special education” no longer appears in the Act.3
